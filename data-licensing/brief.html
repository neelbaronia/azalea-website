Positioning

Sentence-aligned bitext is free. These four things are not.

OPUS, ParaCrawl, CCMatrix and the NLLB bitext releases put billions of segments in the public domain at zero cost. We are not selling volume, and we are not competing on price per word with a translation vendor. What we hold is a credentialed-expert supply in a domain the expert-data marketplaces do not currently cover.

01

Provenance you can defend

Every record carries two rights chains — source text and translation, tracked separately — plus the publication venue, folio and URL. Procurement's binding constraint right now is legal exposure, not quality.

02

No machine output, attested

Each document carries a signed translator attestation: no MT, no LLM drafting, no post-editing. Training on recycled machine output collapses diversity, and a per-document guarantee is worth paying for.

03

An English side nobody has crawled

Our source texts are public and may already sit in web-scale pretraining corpora. Our translations do not. The contamination block states, per record, which side is exposed — which is what makes an evaluation set valid.

04

The translator's reasoning, not just their output

A segment carrying the decision, the rationale in the translator's own words, and the renderings they rejected converts mechanically into a reasoning trace or a preference pair. No scraped corpus can produce this at any volume.

Delivery format

JSONL, one work per line, discourse order preserved

Not TMX or XLIFF — those are CAT-tool interchange formats that force this metadata into <prop> soup and signal language-vendor rather than data supplier. Not a pickle, which no buyer should load from a stranger. Document-level, because a pronoun resolved forty segments earlier is the whole reason literary data is interesting; a flat segment view with context windows ships alongside for drop-in use.

Chosen vs. rejected is already a preference row. The delivered rendering is the chosen completion; each rejected alternative is a plausible, fluent, human-generated dispreferred completion — the hard kind to synthesize, because a model asked to produce a bad translation produces an implausible one.

Beyond the text, each record carries five blocks: translator (name, affiliation, verifiable credential URL), provenance (both rights chains), process (authorship and editorial passes), contamination (public-web exposure per side), and stats. A JSON Schema and a checksummed manifest ship with every delivery.

Commercials

Priced per word and per annotation, not per token

A token is a property of your tokenizer, not of our work — the same passage runs roughly 1.3× more tokens in Chinese than in English under most BPE vocabularies, so per-token pricing penalizes exactly the pairs that are hardest to produce. We price on units that track human effort.

The floor on tier one is our cost: professional literary translation into English runs $0.12–0.20 per word to the translator before editorial. Tier two is derived from credentialed-expert hourly rates at a realistic six to ten considered annotations an hour. Tier three is priced as scarcity rather than effort — an uncontaminated literary eval set in a pair you are weak in has no substitute, and its value collapses the moment it is sold twice.

Terms we hold: non-exclusive by default, exclusivity priced separately; use limited to your own training and evaluation, no redistribution or sublicensing; term-limited with renewal rather than perpetual; translator attribution and revenue share; and no reselling synthetic translations derived from our data as human work.

Status

Rights clearance is in progress, and we will not pretend otherwise

Every record in this sample reads "source_text_license": "NOT CLEARED"

A translation is a derivative work. Copyright in the English belongs to the translator; the right to make and distribute a translation at all belongs to the original author or their magazine. Azalea holds neither in writing today, and the sample is built to say so in the data rather than in a footnote.

This brief is an invitation to price the work and tell us your diligence bar — not an offer of a corpus you can buy this quarter. We would rather sequence it that way than discover at your legal review that we had oversold the position.
